# Institutional AI Maturity Scoring Rubric

**A fixed standard for consistent university benchmarking**
Version 1.0 · October 2026 · [Brilliant AI Solutions](https://brilliantaisolutions.com)

---

## Purpose and governing principle

This rubric operationalizes the seven dimensions of the Brilliant AI Solutions Institutional AI Maturity Benchmarking Framework. It defines the evidence needed to distinguish experimentation from sustainable institutional capability before any university is scored. It contains no university assessments or rankings.

A maturity score describes demonstrated institutional capability. Evidence confidence describes how firmly the public record supports that judgment. Limited disclosure must not be converted into a low maturity score. Where a defensible score cannot be established, use “Not publicly evident.”

The standard is an analyst-designed benchmarking method, not an audit, certification or externally validated maturity instrument. The numeric thresholds and observation periods below are study conventions fixed for consistent application; they are not claims of universal industry standards.

## How to apply the tables

Each level contains three required institutional characteristics. Observable Evidence includes examples of public materials that could substantiate them; the examples are not a required document checklist.

Higher levels retain the relevant strengths of lower levels and add the stated transition requirements. The definitions and next-level blockers establish the boundary above and below each score.

The next-level blockers apply to confirmed conditions. Undocumented requirements must be labeled Not publicly evident, not treated as proven deficiencies.

Use the cross-dimension rules, confidence definitions and calibration procedure together with the tables. Do not select a level from its description alone.

## The five maturity anchors

| Score | Level | Common meaning |
| :-: | :-- | :-- |
| 1 | Initial | Predominantly individual or isolated activity, demonstrated by evidence rather than presumed from silence. |
| 2 | Emerging | Institutional coordination and initial shared support are taking shape. |

## Cross Dimension Scoring Rules

These rules are mandatory for every institution and every dimension. The terms below are study definitions and must not be changed to accommodate a preferred institutional result.

### The scoring unit and institutional boundary

Score the institution across the seven fixed dimensions. Define the included campuses, schools, administrative functions and affiliated health systems before evidence collection. Record an affiliated entity separately unless the assessed capability is available to, governed for, or meaningfully shared with the target institutional population. Use the same boundary logic for every institution.

### Institutional activity versus isolated activity

Institutional capability has an accountable owner or governed federation, a repeatable access or decision pathway, assigned operating capacity, and service reach beyond the originating person or team. Level 2 can recognize an institutional mandate still being implemented. One prominent laboratory, course, grant, conference or chatbot is isolated evidence unless broader ownership, access and continuity are demonstrated.

### Cumulative anchors and score assignment

Each row has three numbered characteristics. Award a whole-number level only when all three are supported, together with the relevant strengths established at lower levels and the essential transition gates. Lower-level weaknesses are not cumulative requirements. Choose the highest fully supported anchor only when the evidence is sufficient to characterize the operating state. Do not average strong and weak indicators within a dimension to bypass a gate.

### Uncertainty is not a maturity level

Use “Not publicly evident” when the evidence cannot support a defensible level. Do not code it as 0, 1 or a midpoint. A confirmed pilot or documented absence of a required process is evidence of a limitation; an unsuccessful search is not. If selected capabilities are demonstrated but the overall operating model is unclear, report the dimension as Not publicly evident and describe those capabilities without assigning a numerical floor as the final score.

### Interpreting the next-level blockers

The last column describes conditions that prevent progression when the conditions are confirmed. If a required capability is merely undocumented, state “Next-level requirement: Not publicly evident.” Do not claim the institution lacks it. A higher level remains unsubstantiated, but a lower score is appropriate only if the lower anchor itself is affirmatively supported.

### Partial evidence and intermediate scores

Use whole numbers by default. An x.5 score is allowed only when all three characteristics at x are established, the essential transition gate for x+1 is met, exactly two of the three characteristics at x+1 are fully supported, and the remaining characteristic is demonstrably partial rather than simply unknown. Record the unmet component and rationale. Never use half scores to average conflicting evidence, express low confidence or reward announcements. Do not use quarter-point scores.

### Decentralized capabilities

A federation can achieve any level. Look for clear responsibilities, interoperable standards, common risk boundaries, local operating capacity, coverage and escalation across units. Do not penalize local delivery if coordination works. Conversely, do not extrapolate one advanced school to the whole institution. For uneven coverage, report the covered domains and verified variation; use Not publicly evident if coverage cannot be established.

### Meaningful adoption

Adoption requires intended users repeatedly using AI in a real academic, research or administrative process, with an accountable owner, support and evidence of persistence. Report the eligible population, active-user definition, usage period and workflow context. License counts, account creation, one-time attendance, page visits and cumulative prompts alone do not establish meaningful adoption. There is no universal percentage threshold across unlike workflows.

### Production status and observation period

A production service supports real work, has an accountable service or process owner, a support path, defined data and risk controls, an operating resource commitment and a change or failure response. For “sustained,” require at least six months or one completed normal operating cycle, whichever is longer; for academic uses, one completed academic term qualifies if it is the normal cycle. A pilot label can be overridden only by explicit evidence that these conditions are met and the trial has graduated. New production services may be operational but not yet sustained.

### Enterprise scale

For this study, enterprise scale means coordinated production use in at least three of four mission domains: administration and operations; teaching and learning; student support and experience; research support and innovation. It must span multiple independently led units and show material coverage of the intended populations, with documented exclusions. Institution-wide reach of a general chatbot alone is insufficient. Apply this threshold directly to Adoption level 4; apply breadth within the relevant domain for the other dimensions.

### Measurable value

Value evidence identifies a baseline or credible comparator, the outcome and unit, observation period, relevant population, method and limitations. Financial or productivity claims include licenses, compute, integration, support, oversight and staff effort. Nonfinancial benefits qualify when measured against mission goals. Separate self-reported benefits from observed results and causally supported effects. Satisfaction and usage can support adoption but cannot alone establish learning gains, net savings or improved service quality.

### Repeated improvement and leading maturity

For level 5, require at least two completed evaluation-and-improvement cycles spanning at least twelve months. Evidence must connect findings to changes and then examine subsequent results. Two reports repeating the same metrics are not two improvement cycles. For slower research or academic cycles, use the longer normal cycle and disclose it. A new initiative cannot receive 5 merely because its design is sophisticated.

### Evidence recency

Use a fixed study cutoff and record publication, update and access dates separately. Prefer operating and adoption evidence from the preceding twelve months. Evidence from thirteen to twenty-four months requires a current service, owner or institutional confirmation of continuity. Older evidence is historical context unless current authoritative material confirms it remains in force. An undated live page shows what is currently represented, not how long it has operated. Access dates and web crawl dates are not implementation dates.

### Announcements and future capabilities

Separate proposed, funded, piloting, newly operational and sustained operation. An announcement supports intent or committed resources, not operational delivery. Credit a documented operating sponsor or allocation process only in the dimension it actually establishes. Do not credit future GPU capacity, planned hires, prospective courses or a committee charter as completed services, staffed capability, academic adoption or functioning reviews.

### Conflicting evidence and source precedence

Resolve conflicts using scope, date and responsibility. Prefer current service-owner documentation for availability and terms, authoritative policy owners for requirements, and accountable institutional reports for results. A newer news release does not automatically supersede a service restriction. Preserve unresolved conflicts, describe the disputed gate and use a score only if the conflict cannot change the anchor; otherwise use Not publicly evident. Do not average the claims.

### Separate the dimensions and avoid prestige bias

Score each dimension independently. Research excellence can coexist with weak administrative adoption. Technology availability cannot establish workforce skill; policy publication cannot establish operational governance; training delivery cannot establish proficiency. A single source may support multiple dimensions only through distinct relevant facts. Institutional fame, budgets, donor gifts, hardware ownership, AI-center counts and website polish earn no independent points.

### Aggregate scores and comparability

When the later study computes an overall score, use the unweighted arithmetic mean of all seven dimensions, as specified in the framework. If any dimension is Not publicly evident, do not publish a directly comparable overall score; show coverage instead. Do not substitute low scores or calculate a six-dimension average as if it were comparable. Retain underlying half-step inputs, display any overall mean to one decimal, and treat small differences as descriptive rather than rankings.

### Evidence records and reviewer discipline

For every proposed score, retain the source URL and title, dates, institutional scope, operating status, concise factual observation, linked characteristic or gate, analyst interpretation, limitations, confidence and reason higher levels are unsubstantiated. Distinguish direct institutional evidence, third-party reporting and analyst inference. Multiple pages repeating one press release count as one underlying evidence item. A website is a source container, not a scored capability.

## Evidence Confidence

Assign confidence after selecting a supported anchor. Confidence never adds points, subtracts points or changes a maturity score. A score of 4 with moderate confidence is a less certain assessment of level 4, not a disguised score of 3.5.

| Confidence | Evidence standard | Interpretation |
| :-: | :-- | :-- |
| High Confidence | Multiple independent, recent primary institutional sources consistently support all score-determining characteristics and gates. At least one source documents operation or results rather than intent. Scope and continuity are clear; no material contradiction remains. | Interpret the score as well substantiated within the public-evidence boundary. This does not certify the institution or eliminate unobserved capability. |
| Moderate Confidence | Reliable primary evidence supports the selected anchor, but coverage, duration or some non-decisive details are incomplete. Older evidence may be corroborated by current service documentation. Limitations do not change the selected level. | Use the score with stated scope and validation needs. Do not lower or increase it because confidence is moderate. |
| Low Confidence | The selected anchor is affirmatively supported but depends on a narrow primary record, corroborated older evidence or indirect reporting. Public coverage is sparse and independent confirmation is limited. No unsupported essential gate may be assumed. | Treat the score as provisional and prioritize validation. If uncertainty affects which anchor is defensible, report Not publicly evident instead of forcing a low-confidence score. |

### When no numeric score is defensible

Use Not publicly evident when essential characteristics cannot be established or material conflicts leave the anchor unresolved. Confidence can describe the limited evidence record, but it cannot rescue an unsupported score. Do not publish “1, Low Confidence” merely because research found little.

### Independent examples

Clear primary records document an owned, limited pilot: an Emerging score can have high confidence.

Current sources document a supported production platform, but non-decisive coverage details are incomplete: an otherwise justified score can have moderate confidence.

Only an announcement of a future platform is available: current operating maturity is Not publicly evident, regardless of the announcement’s credibility.

### Evidence type

Direct institutional evidence is the preferred basis for anchor requirements. Third-party reporting can corroborate dates, scope or outcomes but should be traced to original evidence where possible. Analyst inference must be labeled and cannot substitute for a missing essential transition gate. Interviews or internal documents may validate a later study, but must be disclosed as a different evidence base from public-source benchmarking.

## Calibration Guidance

Calibration checks that the same facts receive the same score. It must not normalize universities toward a desired distribution or create artificial distinctions. Several institutions may legitimately share a score, and some dimensions may remain Not publicly evident.

| Potential inconsistency | Required treatment | Error to avoid |
| :-: | :-- | :-- |
| A highly visible research university has little enterprise evidence | Keep Research separate. Score the research-support service only where access and support are demonstrated. Enterprise dimensions may be Not publicly evident. | Using publications, AI institutes or national reputation to fill enterprise evidence gaps. |
| One school is advanced while others are not documented | Record the school’s scope. Look for institutional access, shared standards and verified coverage before inferring an institutional level. If variation is unknown, do not invent a low university-wide score. | Treating either the strongest school or the least visible school as representative. |
| A coordinated federation has no central AI office | Apply the same ownership, standards, support and outcome tests to the federation. Decentralization is an operating model, not a maturity deficit. | Rewarding organizational centralization as an end in itself. |
| An impressive policy has no evidence of completed reviews | Credit what the policy establishes. Strategy level 3 requires a functioning process, so publication alone cannot satisfy its transition gate. If actual operation is unknown, label that requirement Not publicly evident. | Assuming written requirements are routinely enforced. |
| A campus-wide license has high registration but little repeat use | Technology may be established. Adoption still needs repeat users in real workflows, a period and eligible-population context. | Equating eligibility, licenses or registrations with adoption or value. |
| A service is called a pilot but appears widely used | Check formal operating ownership, support, controls, resources, graduation status and sustained use. Retain pilot status until evidence resolves it; explain any justified reclassification. | Treating marketing labels or user counts alone as proof of production. |
| A new service is already in production | Credit verified operational capability. Do not infer six months of continuity, a completed academic cycle or two improvement cycles from a launch announcement. | Awarding sustained or leading maturity immediately at launch. |
| Thousands of people attend AI training | Assess role coverage, learning goals, applied work, demonstrated proficiency and manager-supported change. Attendance is an activity measure. | Converting participant totals into workforce-capability scores. |
| A new GPU facility has major funding but restricted access | Separate committed resources from operational access. Review eligibility, allocation, support, utilization and continuity; consortium access can be equally mature. | Scoring hardware expenditure or ownership rather than research-enablement capability. |
| A university reports hours saved or improved learning | Check baseline, sample, time period, measurement and attribution. Distinguish self-report from observed results. Include operating and oversight costs when asserting net value. | Treating gross modeled time savings as cash savings or sentiment as learning gain. |
| Current sources disagree about availability or data approval | Identify the service and policy owners, dates and covered populations. If the contradiction changes a gate, use Not publicly evident until resolved. | Selecting the most favorable source or averaging incompatible claims. |
| Two institutions publish very different amounts of detail | Use a consistent search protocol and evidence template. Document search coverage. Treat disclosure differences as confidence and missing-evidence issues, not inherent maturity differences. | Rewarding website quality, communications staffing or search-engine visibility. |
| A mature institution intentionally limits AI in a sensitive workflow | Assess whether the restriction follows risk or learning goals and whether permitted uses are supported effectively. Appropriate non-use can be evidence of maturity. | Assuming more automation, broader data permissions or mandatory AI use is always better. |
| A score appears to fall between levels | Apply the fixed half-step rule and record which two next-level characteristics are established. An unknown third characteristic does not qualify as demonstrably partial. | Using half scores to soften difficult judgments or average conflicting evidence. |

### Calibration procedure

1\. Freeze the rubric as version 1.0, including institutional boundaries, cutoff date and the conventions for duration, enterprise scale and half scores.

2\. Before scoring the peer group, test hypothetical evidence packets covering a policy-only case, a pilot-to-production case, a decentralized model, a research-heavy institution, a sparse-evidence case and a conflicting-source case.

3\. Use independent reviewers when available. Each maps facts to the three characteristics and transition gate, identifies the blocker above the proposed score and assigns confidence separately. No institutional name or reputation should be needed to resolve the hypothetical case.

4\. Reconcile every disagreement affecting an essential gate, every proposed half score, every numeric-versus-Not-publicly-evident disagreement, and any anchor difference of one point or more. Keep the original judgments and the resolution rationale.

5\. If only one reviewer is available, conduct a separate second pass across the same dimension for all institutions. Record the limitation and do not claim inter-rater reliability.

6\. Resolve ambiguity through clarification of the existing standard before full scoring. After scoring begins, any material rubric change requires a new version and reassessment of all affected institutions, not selective adjustment.

7\. In the cross-institution review, compare scope, operating status, duration, coverage and evidence strength before finalizing scores. Do not change a score merely because it appears unexpectedly high or low relative to peers.

## Assessment Record Template

Use one record for each institution and dimension. Preserve factual observations separately from analyst judgments so another reviewer can reproduce the decision.

| Field | Required content |
| :-: | :-- |
| Institution and boundary | Institution name; included campuses, schools and affiliated entities; relevant exclusions. |
| Dimension and cutoff | One of the seven unchanged dimension names; research cutoff and review date. |
| Capability and status | Specific service or process; proposed, funded, pilot, newly operational or sustained; current owner and scope. |
| Source record | URL, title, publisher, publication/update/access dates; primary, third-party or inference; original evidence versus repeated coverage. |
| Characteristic mapping | Facts supporting each of the three characteristics at the proposed level and the essential gate. Mark unsupported requirements Not publicly evident. |
| Coverage and persistence | Units, mission domains, intended population, active use where relevant, operating duration and documented exclusions. |
| Maturity decision | Whole or justified half score, or Not publicly evident; concise anchor rationale. |
| Upper boundary | Confirmed next-level blocker, or next-level requirement Not publicly evident. Distinguish the two explicitly. |
| Confidence | High, Moderate or Low, with source-based rationale independent of the score. |
| Contradictions and validation | Conflicting facts, resolution, remaining questions and evidence needed to revisit the assessment. |
| Reviewer and version | Reviewer, second-pass or second-reviewer outcome, rubric version and adjudication notes. |
